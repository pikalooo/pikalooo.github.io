About

I am currently a Ph.D. student at Soochow University (2023–2027). My research interests include transferable adversarial attacks, submodular optimization and efficient combinatorial optimization, and efficient information management for LLMs. I actively follow the latest advances in artificial intelligence and am particularly interested in leveraging emerging technologies, such as LLMs, to enhance scientific research and address challenging research problems.

Research

🎭

Transferable Adversarial Attacks

My research investigates adversarial security and transferability in deep learning. I study fairness-targeted attacks, robust model learning, and transferable poisoning on graph neural networks and text-attributed graphs. I also develop physical adversarial attacks against modern object detection systems to enhance real-world effectiveness and cross-model transferability.

Submodular Optimization and Efficient Combinatorial Optimization

My research explores efficient optimization and resource allocation in large-scale networks. I develop GNN-based influence maximization methods to improve node selection efficiency, diffusion effectiveness, and generalization. I also design budget-feasible submodular mechanisms that maximize collective utility while encouraging participants to truthfully report their private costs. ﻿

Efficient Information Management for LLMs

My research develops efficient information management for large language models through submodular optimization. I design token-budgeted prompt compression methods that preserve coverage, diversity, and relevance, alongside approximation-guaranteed algorithms for efficient long-term memory selection and dynamic maintenance that reduce computational costs while sustaining downstream task performance.
