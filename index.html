Research

Transferable Adversarial Attacks

My research focuses on adversarial security and transferable attacks against deep learning models. For GNNs, I develop adversarial attack methods based on malicious node injection and fairness saliency optimization to amplify prediction disparities across sensitive groups while preserving overall predictive accuracy as much as possible. I also propose edge-saliency-guided robust GNN learning methods, enabling a unified investigation of adversarial attacks and robustness. Furthermore, I develop transferable poisoning attacks on text-attributed graphs across different model architectures and text encoders. For object detection models, I design physical adversarial attacks that jointly manipulate model attention distributions and classification predictions, aiming to improve attack effectiveness in real-world physical environments and transferability across models.

Submodular Optimization and Efficient Combinatorial Optimization

My research focuses on efficient optimization and resource allocation in large-scale networks. Specifically, I develop GNN-based influence maximization algorithms to improve node selection efficiency, information diffusion effectiveness, and generalization capabilities. In addition, I design budget-feasible submodular optimization mechanisms to select a subset of participants with private participation costs under limited budgets, aiming to maximize overall utility while ensuring incentive compatibility, such that participants are motivated to truthfully report their costs. ﻿

Submodular Optimization and Efficient Combinatorial Optimization
⌁

Efficient Information Management for LLMs

My research focuses on efficient information compression and submodular optimization for LLMs. Specifically, I develop a prompt compression algorithm based on regularized submodular maximization, which jointly considers information coverage, semantic diversity, and query relevance under limited token budgets to improve information retention quality and compression efficiency. In addition, I design novel static and streaming algorithms for knapsack-constrained submodular optimization with theoretical approximation guarantees. These algorithms are further applied to efficiently select and dynamically maintain long-term memory for LLMs, reducing token consumption and computational overhead while preserving downstream task performance.
